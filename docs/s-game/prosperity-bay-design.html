Game design proposal · /s-game

Prosperity Bay

A SimCity-style looped cartoon that explains embedded finance to any audience — a town runs itself on screen while every shop becomes a client, and J.P. Morgan is the invisible infrastructure that makes the money move. No controls required: it plays, it loops, it teaches.

The analogy: a marketplace that levels up like a city

Prosperity Bay is an ambient explainer built on one analogy: the town square is your marketplace. Just as a SimCity village advances to a metropolis when it gets power, water, and roads, the marketplace advances an era every time one embedded-finance capability switches on. Verification turns strangers into trusted merchants; accounts give every stall its own vault; checkout opens the market to the world; payout rails connect it to every merchant's home bank — until the quiet square has become a thriving bazaar, humming on infrastructure the viewer never had to build.

The whole lifecycle animates on its own, chapter by chapter, then seamlessly loops — no line of JSON, no required click. It works full-screen on a conference kiosk, embedded in a landing page, or watched on a phone. Interaction is optional and additive: tapping pauses the town and reveals the real product terms behind the scene.

The metaphor map

Every object in the town corresponds to a real capability from the Embedded Payments and Checkout documentation. This mapping is the backbone of the game — and it appears verbatim in the finale recap.

The loop

Caption sets the scene→ The town animates it→ Benefit banner→ Next chapter↺

Every chapter follows the same rhythm: a one-line caption states a business need, the town animates the answer (construction, coins, vehicles), and a short benefit banner lands the value in plain words ("Verified in hours, not weeks" · "Every coin tracked to its owner" · "Paid at the speed each moment needs"). After the finale, the town gently resets to dawn and the loop begins again — the reset itself is a scene, not a cut.

Advancement is the visible thread. A persistent era badge in the corner names the marketplace's current stage, upgrading with each chapter:

Quiet Square→ Trusted Merchants→ Funded Stalls→ Open Market→ Connected Market→ Thriving Bazaar

The six chapters

Chapter 1 · Dawn

Welcome to Prosperity Bay

"You run the town. You don't have to build the bank."

Scene
Sunrise over an almost-empty map: a road, the bay, and the bank tower on the hill already glowing. The caption introduces the premise — this town is your platform, and the tower is the single provider whose machinery everything else will quietly run on.
Banner
"Grow your ecosystem — everything your businesses need, without leaving the platform."
Length
~20 seconds.
Chapter 2 · Onboarding

Open the Doors

"Rosie's Bakery wants to set up shop."

Scene
A little cart rolls in and hands City Hall an application folder. Papers flutter in — business details, owner ID, tax number. A status ribbon over City Hall animates through Received → Under review → One more paper → Approved!, mirroring the real client lifecycle (NEW → REVIEW_IN_PROGRESS → INFORMATION_REQUESTED → APPROVED); the missing-paper beat resolves itself in seconds to show it's a normal, recoverable step, not a rejection.
On screen
On approval the bakery constructs itself on its lot with a confetti pop, and the town's trust meter fills a notch.
Banner
"Digital-first onboarding with verification built in — trusted from day one."

Chapter 3 · Manage Funds

Every Shop Gets a Vault

"Rosie needs somewhere for her money to live."

Scene
A glowing vault slides in beneath the freshly built bakery and a golden pipe connects it to the town's money grid. A small ledger pops up reading $0.00, ready to count. A cutaway shows the vault is hers alone — money is never pooled in a shoebox.
Banner
"Every business gets its own account — every coin tracked to its owner, up to the minute."

Chapter 4 · Accept Payments

Ring the Register

"Rosie is ready to sell — online and everywhere."

Scene
Three customers arrive in sequence, one per checkout style, each labeled with a floating tag: one pays at the built-in counter (a form on the shop's own site — Drop-in UI), one at the market stall (a ready-made page — Hosted Payment Page), and one redeems a delivery coupon from their phone (Pay by Link).
On screen
Each card briefly turns into a lockbox (tokenization — the shop never holds the real card). One customer taps their phone to confirm identity (3-D Secure); one shady visitor is politely turned away at the security gate (fraud screening). Coins arc into Rosie's vault and the ledger ticks up.
Banner
"Accept payments your customers' way — frictionless in front, fraud-screened and secure behind."

Chapter 5 · Transfer Funds

Money in Motion

"Rosie wants her earnings in her own bank."

Scene
A little "home bank" cottage appears at the town edge and the town pins its address to the map (saving a recipient). Then three payouts depart at once, each labeled with its need and vehicle: Friday payday, needed now rides the express monorail (instant, small fee — real-time payments); the weekly settlement rides the cargo train (low cost, arrives next day — ACH); a big payment to the oven supplier goes by armored courier (same-day, high value — wire).
On screen
Coins board each vehicle and travel their routes at speeds matching the rail — the monorail arrives while the train is still rolling.
Banner
"Transfer funds at the speed each moment needs — instant, overnight, or high-value."
Transfer FundsPayoutsRecipientsACHReal-time paymentsWire
Chapter 6 · The Thriving Bazaar

The Marketplace Runs Itself

"Word has spread — and the town barely lifts a finger."

Scene
A florist, a gym, a bookshop, and a coffee cart all arrive at once. Everything from Chapters 2–5 now replays as a fast, joyful montage: permits approve, vaults slide in, registers ring, vehicles roll — the same lifecycle, automatic and at scale. Each event rings the city bell and pins a note to the notice board ("Florist approved!" · "Payment received" · "Payout delivered") — the webhook metaphor: the town tells you the moment something happens, so nobody has to keep checking.
Finale
The camera pulls back on the full bazaar at golden hour while a prosperity dashboard rises: money in, money out, businesses served. A closing card shows the metaphor map beside the real capabilities with a "Build this for real" pointer to the docs — then dusk falls, the map clears, and the loop returns to dawn.
Banner
"Accept, manage, and transfer funds — end to end, from a single provider, at any scale."

Interaction model — all optional

  • Nothing is required. The cartoon plays and loops unattended — kiosk-safe by default.
  • Tap/click anywhere pauses the town and opens the current chapter's detail card with its glossary chips, which flip between town language ("permit review") and product language ("client onboarding / KYC"). Resume is automatic after a few idle seconds.
  • Chapter dots along the bottom show loop progress and allow jumping straight to a chapter — handy when a presenter wants to talk over one scene.
  • Reduced motion swaps travel animations for crossfades; captions and banners carry the full story on their own, so the piece also works with animations effectively off.
  • All copy — captions, banners, chips — lives in one content file for easy editing and future localization.

Visual & theme system

Flat-isometric SVG on a 2:1 diamond grid — the clean, sunny SimCity look. Buildings are simple extruded blocks with flat faces and soft shadows; framer-motion springs handle construction pops, coin arcs, and vehicle travel. Reduced-motion users get crossfades instead of travel animations.

Meadow Bay water Shop coral Plaster Bank navy Coin gold

Every color runs through CSS custom properties (--sg-*) scoped under the game's root class, so a future theme — a night-neon town, a blueprint sketch — is just an alternate token set plus swapped SVG palettes. The theme switcher is scaffolded from day one but ships with the single "Sunny Bay" theme.

Mobile-first layout

  • Portrait phones: the scene fills the screen; the caption and benefit banner sit in a slim strip along the bottom, sized for reading at arm's length. The camera does the work — it drifts and zooms to whatever the chapter is about, so nothing ever needs pinching.
  • Landscape / desktop / kiosk: the scene breathes wide; captions move to a lower-third band, era badge top-left, chapter dots bottom-center.
  • The iso scene is one SVG with a responsive viewBox — crisp at any size, no raster assets, safe to leave running for hours.

Architecture — fully isolated at /s-game

A self-contained route and component tree. No imports from the showcase, portal themes, or the fortress game; the only shared things are libraries already in the app (React, zustand, framer-motion). Styling is the game's own CSS file with sg- prefixed classes and tokens.

src/routes/s-game.tsx              → mounts <SGameApp />
src/components/s-game/
  SGameApp.tsx                     shell: scene + caption strip + chapter dots
  s-game.css                       scoped tokens & styles (.sg-root)
  content/script.ts                every caption, banner, and chip in one place
  engine/timeline.ts               chapter/beat scheduler: play, pause, loop, jump
  engine/store.ts                  zustand store: current chapter, era, pause state
  chapters/ch1-dawn.ts … ch6-bazaar.ts   declarative beat lists per chapter
  scene/IsoScene.tsx               SVG isometric renderer (tiles, roads, camera)
  scene/buildings.tsx              bakery, florist, gym, bank tower, city hall…
  scene/actors.tsx                 customers, cart, train, monorail, courier, coins
  ui/CaptionStrip.tsx              caption + benefit banner band
  ui/EraBadge.tsx                  marketplace advancement badge
  ui/GlossaryChip.tsx              town-term ⇄ product-term flip chip
  ui/ChapterDots.tsx               loop progress / jump-to-chapter

The heart is the timeline engine: each chapter is a declarative list of timed beats (show caption → move actor → play effect → show banner) that the scheduler runs, pauses, and loops. Framer-motion animates the beats; the same data drives the reduced-motion crossfade variant. No video files — the whole cartoon is code, so it stays sharp, lightweight, theme-able, and easy to edit line by line.

Build plan

  • Milestone 1 — first loop: route, iso scene, timeline engine, Chapters 1–2 playing and looping end to end.
  • Milestone 2: Chapters 3–5 (vault, checkout, payout rails) with all actors and effects.
  • Milestone 3: Chapter 6 montage, dashboard finale, dusk-to-dawn loop seam, reduced-motion pass, mobile QA.
  • Milestone 4: pause-to-explore details, glossary chips, theme-switcher scaffold, polish.

Open questions

  • Naming — is "Prosperity Bay" a keeper, or should the town carry a name closer to your brand/demo family?
  • Branding rules — how explicitly can the bank tower say "J.P. Morgan" in a public demo, and should the finale point to the public docs?
  • Narration — captions only, or also subtle ambient sound (register ding, city bell), off by default for kiosk use?
  • Loop pacing — is ~3 minutes right for the venues you have in mind (booth screens favor shorter, landing pages tolerate longer)?
  • Analytics — should chapters emit simple telemetry (viewed, paused, chip-flipped) for demo insight?
  • Languages — copy is centralized either way; is localization on the roadmap?
